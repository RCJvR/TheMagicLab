The Cell — Unit of Life

Every living thing is made of cells. Explore the structure of plant and animal cells and discover what each part does.

Cell Theory

The cell theory is one of the fundamental principles of biology. It states three things:

  • All living organisms are made of one or more cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of life — the smallest unit that can carry out all life processes.
  • All cells come from pre-existing cells (cells only arise from other cells).
Unicellular organisms (like amoeba and bacteria) consist of a single cell. Multicellular organisms (like humans and trees) are made of trillions of specialised cells working together.

Parts found in ALL cells

  • Cell membrane — a thin, flexible layer that surrounds every cell. It controls what enters and exits the cell.
  • Cytoplasm — a jelly-like fluid that fills the cell and holds the organelles in place.
  • Nucleus — the control centre of the cell. Contains DNA, which carries the genetic instructions for the cell.
  • Mitochondria — the "powerhouse" of the cell. Releases energy from food through cellular respiration.
  • Ribosomes — tiny structures where proteins are made.

Why are cells so small?

Cells are microscopic because they need a large surface area to volume ratio. A smaller cell can exchange materials (oxygen, nutrients, waste) across its membrane more efficiently relative to its size. If cells were too large, their centres would not receive enough oxygen.

Specialised cells have specific shapes that suit their function. Red blood cells are disc-shaped to carry oxygen. Nerve cells are long and thin to transmit electrical signals. Root hair cells have a large surface area to absorb water.
