Biodiversity of Animals

Of the roughly 30 animal phyla, six are studied in depth here. Compare their body plans — symmetry, tissue layers, gut structure, and coelom — and see how invertebrates support agriculture and ecosystems.

The concept of a phylum

A phylum is a major grouping in animal classification, based on shared body plan features. Of the roughly 30 animal phyla, six are studied here: Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, and Chordata.

Comparing body plans

Four key features let us compare body plans across phyla:

  • Symmetry and cephalisation — whether the body is symmetrical (and how), and whether there's a concentration of nerve tissue forming a head.
  • Number of tissue layers developed from the embryo (e.g. two layers vs three).
  • Number of openings in the gut — one opening serving as both mouth and anus, or two separate openings.
  • Coelom and blood systems — whether a fluid-filled body cavity (coelom) and a circulatory system are present.

Invertebrates matter — in agriculture and ecosystems

Invertebrates (animals without a backbone, which includes five of the six phyla studied here) play essential roles that are easy to overlook:

  • Pollination — many arthropods (like bees) pollinate crops and wild plants.
  • Decomposition — annelids (like earthworms) and other invertebrates break down organic matter.
  • Soil aeration — burrowing invertebrates like earthworms improve soil structure and aeration.
