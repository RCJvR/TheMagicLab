Responding to the Environment — Nervous System & Senses

Nerve impulses race through your body at incredible speed, letting you react to danger before you've even consciously noticed it. Explore how neurons, the brain, and your eyes and ears work together.

Neuron structure and the nerve impulse

A neuron (nerve cell) is the basic functional unit of the nervous system, adapted to carry electrical impulses rapidly around the body. Its main parts are:

  • Dendrites — branching extensions that receive impulses from other neurons and carry them toward the cell body.
  • Cell body — contains the nucleus and most organelles.
  • Axon — a long fibre that carries the impulse away from the cell body toward the next neuron or an effector (muscle or gland).
  • Synapse — the small gap between the axon of one neuron and the dendrite of the next. When an impulse arrives, chemicals called neurotransmitters are released, diffuse across the gap, and trigger a new impulse in the next neuron.

Organisation of the nervous system

The human nervous system is divided into two main parts:

  • Central nervous system (CNS) — the brain and spinal cord; this is where information is processed and responses are coordinated.
  • Peripheral nervous system (PNS) — all the nerves that branch out from the CNS to the rest of the body, carrying impulses to and from the CNS.

The reflex arc

A reflex action is a fast, automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus that does not require conscious thought — for example, pulling your hand away from something hot. The pathway an impulse follows is called a reflex arc:

Stimulus → receptor → sensory neuron → (relay neuron in spinal cord) → motor neuron → effector → response

Because the impulse can bypass the brain (being processed at the spinal cord instead), reflexes are much faster than responses that require conscious decision-making.

The eye — structure and the pathway of light

The eye detects light and converts it into nerve impulses that the brain interprets as sight:

  • Cornea — the transparent front layer that does most of the initial bending (refraction) of light entering the eye.
  • Lens — fine-tunes the focus, bending light further so it falls precisely on the retina.
  • Retina — the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye, containing receptor cells (rods and cones) that convert light into nerve impulses.
  • Optic nerve — carries these impulses from the retina to the brain for interpretation.
Light pathway: cornea → pupil → lens → (light focused onto) retina → optic nerve → brain.

The ear — hearing and balance

The ear has three regions, each playing a different role:

RegionKey structures & function
Outer earPinna (ear flap) collects sound waves and funnels them down the ear canal to the eardrum.
Middle earThe eardrum vibrates; three tiny bones (ossicles) amplify these vibrations and transmit them to the inner ear.
Inner earThe cochlea converts vibrations into nerve impulses (sent via the auditory nerve to the brain) for hearing. The semi-circular canals detect changes in head position and movement, providing the sense of balance.
