Homeostasis: carbon dioxide, salts and thyroxine

Negative feedback beyond temperature and glucose: how the medulla oblongata controls carbon dioxide, how aldosterone balances salts, and how TSH and thyroxine regulate metabolism.

Negative feedback: the pattern

Every homeostatic mechanism has the same steps: a change from the normal level is detected by receptors, a control centre sends a message to effectors, and the effectors reverse the change. This is negative feedback.

Exam tip: describe each mechanism in this order — stimulus, receptor, control centre, message (nerve or hormone), effector, response, and return to normal.

Carbon dioxide

  1. During exercise, cells respire faster and the carbon dioxide level in the blood rises.
  2. Receptors in the carotid arteries and aorta (and in the medulla itself) detect the increase.
  3. The medulla oblongata sends impulses to the breathing muscles (diaphragm and intercostal muscles) and the heart.
  4. Breathing becomes faster and deeper, and the heart rate increases.
  5. More carbon dioxide is exhaled, so the level returns to normal.

Salt balance: aldosterone

  1. When the sodium ion level in the blood falls, the adrenal cortex secretes more aldosterone.
  2. Aldosterone causes the kidney tubules to reabsorb more sodium ions into the blood.
  3. The sodium level returns to normal.
  4. When the sodium level is too high, less aldosterone is secreted, so less sodium is reabsorbed and more is excreted in urine.

Water balance is controlled separately by ADH from the pituitary gland, which makes the collecting ducts more permeable to water.

Thyroxine and TSH

  1. When the thyroxine level is low, the hypothalamus stimulates the pituitary gland to secrete TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone).
  2. TSH stimulates the thyroid gland to secrete thyroxine, which increases the metabolic rate.
  3. When thyroxine rises above normal, it inhibits TSH secretion, so the thyroid secretes less thyroxine.

HyperthyroidismToo much thyroxineHigh metabolic rate, weight loss, nervousness, fast heart rate
HypothyroidismToo little thyroxineLow metabolic rate, weight gain, tiredness

Worked example: explaining a feedback loop

✏️ Why iodine deficiency causes goitre
1Without iodine, the thyroid cannot make enough thyroxine.
2Low thyroxine no longer inhibits the pituitary, so TSH secretion stays high.
3TSH keeps stimulating the thyroid, which grows larger: a goitre.
