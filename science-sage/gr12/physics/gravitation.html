Newton’s law of universal gravitation

Calculate the gravitational force between masses, the gravitational acceleration on a planet’s surface and at a height, and explain the difference between mass and weight.

The law

📖 Definition (learn the wording)

Newton’s law of universal gravitation: every particle with mass in the universe attracts every other particle with a force which is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

F = G m₁m₂ ÷ r²   G = 6,67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²·kg⁻²

  • The force is always attractive, and the two masses exert equal and opposite forces on each other (Newton’s third law).
  • r is measured between the centres of the masses — for a planet, from the centre of the planet.

Proportional reasoning

Many multiple-choice questions ask what happens to F when a quantity changes. Use the formula as a ratio:

ChangeEffect on F
One mass doubledF doubles
Both masses doubledF × 4
Distance doubledF ÷ 4
Distance tripledF ÷ 9
Distance halvedF × 4

Gravitational acceleration and weight

On a planet of mass M and radius R, the weight of an object is the gravitational force on it, so mg = GMm ÷ R² and

📖 Gravitational acceleration

g = GM ÷ r² (r = R at the surface; r = R + h at a height h above the surface)

✏️ Worked example 1: g on Earth
1M = 5,98 × 10²⁴ kg, R = 6,38 × 10⁶ m.
2g = (6,67 × 10⁻¹¹)(5,98 × 10²⁴) ÷ (6,38 × 10⁶)² = 9,80 m·s⁻².
✏️ Worked example 2: at a height of 2R
1Distance from the centre = R + 2R = 3R.
2g′ = GM ÷ (3R)² = (1/9) × GM/R² = g ÷ 9.

Mass and weight

  • Mass is the amount of matter in an object (kg). It is the same everywhere.
  • Weight is the gravitational force the Earth (or another planet) exerts on an object (N): w = mg. It depends on where the object is.
✏️ Worked example 3: force between two objects
1Two 1 000 kg cars are 2 m apart (between centres).
2F = (6,67 × 10⁻¹¹)(1 000)(1 000) ÷ 2² = 1,67 × 10⁻⁵ N — far too small to notice, which is why gravity between everyday objects is usually ignored.
