Motion graphs for vertical motion and bounces

Sketch and read position–time, velocity–time and acceleration–time graphs for a ball that is thrown, falls and bounces, and use gradients and areas to calculate heights and speeds.

Choosing a sign convention

Choose up (or down) as positive and keep it for the whole question. Taking up as positive: the acceleration due to gravity is −9,8 m·s⁻² at all times while the ball is in the air, whether it is moving up, at the top, or moving down.

A ball that bounces

A ball dropped onto the floor (up positive):

  • Falling: velocity becomes more negative along a straight line of gradient −9,8; position decreases along a curve.
  • At the bounce: velocity changes from negative to positive almost instantly — on the v–t graph this is a nearly vertical jump. The rebound speed is smaller than the impact speed if the bounce is inelastic.
  • Rising: velocity decreases from its positive value to zero at the top, along a line with the same gradient (−9,8) as before — the lines are parallel.
  • Acceleration–time: a horizontal line at −9,8 m·s⁻², interrupted by a short, very large positive spike during contact with the floor, when the floor exerts a large upward force.

Worked example: a dropped ball

✏️ Ball dropped from 1,8 m, rebounds at 4,0 m·s⁻¹
1Speed at impact: vf² = vi² + 2aΔy = 0 + 2(9,8)(1,8) → vf = 5,94 m·s⁻¹ downwards.
2Time to fall: Δt = Δv/a = 5,94 ÷ 9,8 = 0,61 s.
3Height after the bounce: 0 = 4,0² − 2(9,8)Δy → Δy = 0,82 m.
4The rebound speed (4,0 m·s⁻¹) is less than the impact speed (5,94 m·s⁻¹), so kinetic energy was lost: the bounce is inelastic.
Graph-sketching checklist: label both axes with quantities and units; mark known values (times, velocities); draw parallel lines for the free-fall sections; show the velocity jumping from negative to positive at the bounce; make the second peak lower if the bounce is inelastic.

Question 1 · (3 marks)

Question 2 · (6 marks)
A ball is dropped from a height of 2,5 m and rebounds with a speed of 5 m·s⁻¹. (a) Calculate the speed just before impact. (b) Calculate the maximum height after the bounce. (c) Is the bounce elastic? Explain.
Show memo
(a) vf² = 0 + 2(9,8)(2,5) ✓ → vf = 7 m·s⁻¹ ✓
(b) 0 = 5² − 2(9,8)Δy ✓ → Δy = 1,28 m ✓
(c) Inelastic ✓: the rebound speed is less than the impact speed, so kinetic energy is lost ✓.
Question 3 · (4 marks)
Sketch a velocity–time graph (up positive) for the ball in question 2, from release until it reaches its maximum height after the bounce. Label the key values.
Show memo

Question 4 · (3 marks)
Explain why the acceleration–time graph shows a large positive spike at the instant of the bounce.
Show memo
During contact the floor exerts a large upward normal force on the ball ✓ for a very short time ✓, so the net force and therefore the acceleration are large and upward (positive) ✓.
