History of Life on Earth

Life has existed on Earth for billions of years, shaped by dramatic events — explosions of new life forms and mass extinctions. Explore the geological timescale and its key turning points.

The geological timescale

Scientists divide Earth's long history into a geological timescale, split into three broad eras (you don't need to memorise exact dates or every period name):

EraRoughly
Paleozoic"Ancient life" — began around 541 million years ago (Mya)
Mesozoic"Middle life" — the age of dinosaurs
Cenozoic"New life" — the current era, since 65 Mya

South Africa has a rich fossil record documenting key events in this history, including trilobites in the Karoo, and evidence of changing sea levels along the coast.

The Cambrian Explosion

Around 541 Mya, a relatively short period called the Cambrian Explosion saw the rapid appearance of most major animal groups still around today — giving huge insight into the origins of animal diversity.

Mass extinctions

Throughout Earth's history, there have been five mass extinctions — events where a large percentage of species died out relatively quickly. Two are especially important to know:

  • ~250 Mya (Permian extinction) — around 90% of all life on Earth went extinct. Hypotheses for the cause include a massive volcanism event and/or a meteorite impact.
  • 65 Mya (Cretaceous extinction) — wiped out many species, including the (non-bird) dinosaurs. The leading explanation is a giant meteorite impact.

Recent changes and the "sixth extinction"

In just the last four million years, significant changes have occurred in species found in Africa — including the evolution of humans (studied further in Grade 11 and 12). Today, scientists warn that the current rate of extinction is higher than at almost any point in the past, largely due to human activity — sometimes called the sixth extinction.
