Support Systems in Animals

Animals need support to move on land against gravity. Compare the three animal skeleton types, then explore the human skeleton and how joints let bones move.

Why do animals need support?

Unlike plants, most animals move around actively — and life on land in particular requires a way to resist gravity. Support systems provide support, protection, movement, and in vertebrates, mineral storage and even a role in hearing (the tiny bones of the middle ear).

The human skeleton

The human endoskeleton is divided into two main parts:

  • Axial skeleton — the skull (cranium and facial bones), including the foramen magnum (where the spinal cord exits) and the palate and jaws.
  • Appendicular skeleton — the bones of the limbs (arms and legs) and the girdles that attach them to the axial skeleton.
Functions of the human skeleton: support, protection (of organs like the brain and heart), movement (bones act as levers for muscles), storage of minerals (like calcium), and even hearing (three tiny bones in the middle ear transmit sound vibrations).

Bones and joints

A typical long bone has a hard outer layer and, depending on the bone, a store of marrow inside. Where bones meet at a joint, several tissues work together:

  • Cartilage — a smooth, slightly flexible tissue that cushions the ends of bones where they meet.
  • Ligaments — tough tissue connecting bone to bone, holding the joint together.
  • Tendons — tough tissue connecting muscle to bone, allowing muscle contraction to move the bone.
