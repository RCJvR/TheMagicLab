Motion in One Dimension

Analyse the motion of objects using displacement, velocity and acceleration — and apply the equations of motion to solve kinematic problems.

Instantaneous vs Average: Average velocity = total displacement / total time. Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at a specific moment — it equals the slope of a position-time graph at that point.

Motion Graphs

Position-time (x-t) graph:

  • Slope = velocity
  • Horizontal line → object is stationary (v = 0)
  • Straight line (non-zero slope) → constant velocity
  • Curved line → changing velocity (acceleration)

Velocity-time (v-t) graph:

  • Slope = acceleration
  • Area under the graph = displacement
  • Horizontal line → constant velocity (a = 0)
  • Straight line with positive slope → uniform positive acceleration
  • Line crossing v = 0 → object reverses direction

Acceleration-time (a-t) graph:

  • Area under the graph = change in velocity (Δv)
  • Horizontal line → constant (uniform) acceleration
For uniform acceleration: v-t graph is a straight line. The area of the trapezium under v-t gives displacement.

Equations of Motion

These four equations apply only when acceleration is uniform (constant). Always define your positive direction first.

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t

Free Fall

Free fall is motion under gravity alone — no air resistance. The acceleration due to gravity near Earth's surface is g = 9.8 m·s⁻² downward.

Sign convention for free fall: Choose upward as positive → g = −9.8 m·s⁻² (or choose downward as positive → g = +9.8 m·s⁻²). Be consistent throughout your calculation.

Worked example: A ball is thrown upward at 14.7 m·s⁻¹. How high does it go?
Let up = +; u = +14.7 m·s⁻¹; a = −9.8 m·s⁻²; v = 0 at highest point.
v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 14.7² + 2(−9.8)s → s = 216.09/19.6 ≈ 11.0 m
At the highest point of a projectile thrown upward: v = 0 but a = g downward (≠ 0).

Ticker Tape Analysis

A ticker timer makes 50 dots per second (period = 0.02 s). The spacing between dots gives information about velocity and acceleration:

  • Equal spacing → constant velocity
  • Increasing spacing → acceleration (speeding up)
  • Decreasing spacing → deceleration (slowing down)
IEB Extension — Deriving Equations & Non-uniform Acceleration

Deriving from first principles: Starting from a = Δv/Δt = (v − u)/t, rearrange to get v = u + at. Substituting into s = average velocity × time: s = ½(u + v)t = ½(u + u + at)t = ut + ½at². Eliminate t between these two to get v² = u² + 2as.

Multi-stage problems: An object thrown upward from the edge of a cliff may have different conditions on the way up vs. the way down. Split the problem into stages, using the final velocity of stage 1 as the initial velocity of stage 2. Example: A ball thrown up at 10 m·s⁻¹ from a 15 m cliff. Find the time to hit the ground. (Take up as positive, displacement = −15 m at ground level.)

Non-uniform acceleration (qualitative): When acceleration varies with time, the equations of motion no longer apply. The instantaneous acceleration equals the slope of the v-t graph at each point. Displacement is still the area under the v-t graph (found by counting squares or integration in higher grades).
