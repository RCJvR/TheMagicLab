Strengthening Materials

Make paper super strong

A flat sheet of paper is floppy. Fold it or roll it, and it can hold up books!

1 Folding makes paper stronger

A flat sheet of paper bends easily. If you fold it, the same sheet becomes much stronger.

FlatFloppy: it can hardly hold a coin
FoldedA fold or folded-up edges make it stiffer
ZigzagMany folds, like the inside of a cardboard box, make it very strong
Investigate

Paper bridge

Make a bridge between two books with a sheet of paper. How many coins can it hold? Now fold the paper into a zigzag and try again. Record your results.

2 Hollow pillars

We can fold paper into hollow pillars that are circular, triangular or square. Which shape holds the most weight?

Circular pillarRoll the sheet into a tube and tape it
Triangular pillarFold the sheet into three equal parts and tape it
Square pillarFold the sheet into four equal parts and tape it
Bar graphDraw your results so you can compare them
Fair test

Which pillar is the strongest?

  1. Use three sheets of the same paper. Make one circular, one triangular and one square pillar.
  2. Stand each pillar up and carefully put books on top, one at a time.
  3. Count how many books each pillar holds before it crumples.
  4. Draw a bar graph of your results.

3 Paper struts

We can roll paper into long, thin tubes called struts. Struts are strong and light, and we join them to build frames.

Roll itRoll a sheet tightly around a pencil, from one corner
Tape itTape the end so it does not unroll, then slide the pencil out
Thicker is strongerTighter rolls and more layers make a stronger strut
Make it

Make ten struts

Roll ten paper struts. Keep them for the next lesson, where you will join them into strong frame structures.
