Rocket Systems

3, 2, 1… lift-off!

Rockets carry people and machines into space. Find out how a rocket pushes itself up, then design, launch and test your own balloon rocket.

1 Rockets and space travel

People have used rockets to go into space and to travel to the Moon. Rockets also carry satellites that help us with weather, phones and maps.

1957A rocket launches Sputnik 1, the first satellite
1961Yuri Gagarin is the first person in space
1969Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walk on the Moon
2002Mark Shuttleworth is the first South African in space
Read and share

Space heroes

Find out about one person who has travelled into space. When did they go? What did they do there? Share three facts with the class.

2 A rocket is a system

A rocket is a system used to push vehicles into space. It burns fuel to make hot exhaust gases. The rocket moves by pushing the gases out through its back end. The gases go down, so the rocket goes up!

InputFuel
ProcessThe fuel burns. Hot gases are pushed out of the back.
OutputThe rocket moves forward (up)
Like a balloonLet go of a blown-up balloon: air rushes out one way, the balloon flies the other way
Try it

Balloon blast

Blow up a balloon and hold the end closed. Let it go. Which way does the air go? Which way does the balloon go?

3 Design a balloon rocket

Design, make and evaluate a balloon rocket. Measure how far it travels and draw a bar graph to compare different rockets.

You needBalloons of different sizes, drinking straws, fishing line, sticky tape, a measuring tape
MeasureHow many metres did it travel along the line?
Bar graphOne bar for each rocket you test
EvaluateWhich went furthest? Why? How can you make it better?
Project

Balloon rocket race

  1. Thread a straw onto fishing line and tie the line tightly between two chairs.
  2. Blow up a balloon, hold it closed and tape it to the straw.
  3. Let go! Measure how far it travelled.
  4. Test different balloons. Draw a bar graph of your results and evaluate.
