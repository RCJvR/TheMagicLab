The Moon

Watch the Moon

The Moon is a ball of rock with no air and no water. Find out why its shape seems to change every night, and keep your own Moon watch.

1 Features of the Moon

The Moon is a ball of rock in space. There is no air and no water on the Moon. It is smaller than the Earth, and much closer to us than the Sun.

Rock and cratersGrey rock, dust and round holes called craters
No air, no waterNothing can live there. Astronauts must take air with them.
SmallerAbout 4 Moons fit side by side across the Earth
CloserAbout 384 000 km away. The Sun is about 400 times further.
Investigate

Make craters

Fill a tray with flour and sprinkle cocoa on top. Drop small stones and marbles from different heights. What do the holes look like? Which made the biggest crater?

2 Phases of the Moon

The Moon does not make its own light. The Sun's light shines on it. We can only see the part of the Moon that the sunlight shines on. This changing shape is called the phases of the Moon. The pattern repeats every 29½ days, about a month.

New moonThe lit side faces away from us. We cannot see the Moon.
CrescentA thin curve of light
QuarterHalf of the round face is lit
Full moonThe whole round face is lit
Demonstrate

Lamp and ball

In a dark room, a lamp is the Sun. Hold a ball (the Moon) at arm's length and turn slowly in a circle. Watch how much of the ball is lit as you turn.

3 Moon watch and Moon stories

Keep a Moon watch: draw the Moon every night for a month. For thousands of years people have used the Moon to count months, plan planting and tell stories.

The Moon and the Hare

A Khoikhoi story tells how the Moon sent the Hare with a message to people: “As I die and come back to life, so you will also die and live again.” But the Hare got the message wrong and told people: “As I die and do not come back, so you will die and not live again.” The Moon was so angry that it hit the Hare on the mouth. That, says the story, is why the hare has a split lip.

Moon watch

My Moon diary

  1. Every evening for a month, look at the Moon at the same time.
  2. Draw its shape in a calendar block and write the date.
  3. On some days you will not see it. Write down why (clouds, or it is not up yet).
  4. After a month, look at your pattern. When was it full?
