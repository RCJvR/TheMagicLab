Solid Materials

From nature to useful

Glass, paper and plastic all start as raw materials from nature. What are they made from, and what are they like?

1 Raw materials become useful materials

Raw materials come from nature. People change them into manufactured materials that we use every day.

Sand → glassSand is melted to make glass
Clay → ceramicsClay is shaped and baked into mugs, plates and tiles
Coal and oil → plasticAlso paints and some fabrics
Wood and plant fibre → paperCotton plants give fibre for fabric too
Wool and hide → fabric and leatherFrom sheep and cattle
Material detectives

What is it made from?

Find five objects in the classroom. For each one, write the manufactured material it is made of, and the raw material that came from nature. Example: window → glass → sand.

2 How paper is made

Paper is made from plant fibres, mostly from wood. The fibres are mashed with water, spread thin, pressed and dried.

TreesLogs are cut into tiny wood chips
PulpChips are mashed with water into a mushy pulp
Press and dryThin layers are pressed and dried into paper
RecycleOld paper can be made into new paper, saving trees
Make it

Recycled paper

  1. Tear old newspaper into small pieces and soak them in water overnight.
  2. Mash them into pulp (an adult can use a blender).
  3. Spread the pulp thinly on a piece of fly screen.
  4. Press out the water with a sponge and leave it to dry in the sun.

3 Properties of materials

Every material has properties: what it is like. We choose a material because its properties suit the job.

Hard or softCan you scratch or dent it?
Stiff or flexibleDoes it bend?
Strong or weakDoes it break or tear easily?
Light or heavyHow heavy is a piece of the same size?
Waterproof or absorbentDoes water run off or soak in?
Test it

Material tests

Test pieces of paper, fabric, plastic, wood and leather. Scratch them, bend them, pull them, weigh them and drip water on them. Record your results in a table. Which would make the best raincoat? Why?
