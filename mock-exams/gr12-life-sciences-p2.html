Protein synthesis (max 11)
Transcription takes place in the nucleus ✓
The DNA double helix unwinds ✓
The weak hydrogen bonds break and the strands separate ✓
One strand is used as a template ✓
Free RNA nucleotides attach by complementary base pairing ✓
(A with U, C with G) ✓
forming mRNA ✓
The mRNA leaves the nucleus through a nuclear pore ✓
and attaches to a ribosome ✓
Each tRNA carries a specific amino acid ✓
The anticodon on the tRNA matches the codon on the mRNA ✓
The amino acids are joined by peptide bonds ✓
to form a protein (polypeptide) with a specific sequence of amino acids ✓

Gene mutations (max 6)
A gene mutation is a change in the sequence of bases in the DNA ✓
for example the substitution, insertion or deletion of a base ✓
The codon on the mRNA changes ✓
A different tRNA brings a different amino acid ✓
The sequence of amino acids in the protein changes ✓
so the protein may have a different shape and may not function properly ✓
If the new codon codes for the same amino acid, the protein does not change (silent mutation) ✓
Example: sickle-cell anaemia, where one amino acid in haemoglobin is changed ✓
